What’s happening?

Canada’s money news, explained plainly. Stories from late October to mid-November 2026.

Economy · Canada Oct 28, 2026

Bank of Canada raises its key interest rate to 2.5%

The central bank raised its benchmark rate by a quarter of a percentage point, as markets expected. It had held the rate at 2.25% for seven meetings in a row.

The key rate sets how expensive it is for banks to borrow, which flows through to mortgages, car loans and credit cards. Raising it makes borrowing cost more, which slows spending and helps cool rising prices. Savers can earn a little more, but borrowers pay more.

EconomyOct 26, 2026

Inflation climbs to 3.1% as gas prices rise

Statistics Canada reported that annual inflation rose in September, with the war in the Middle East pushing up gasoline prices.

Inflation is how fast everyday prices rise. At 3.1%, something that cost $100 a year ago now costs about $103. That is one reason the Bank of Canada raised rates.
Source: BNN Bloomberg ↗
MarketsOct 28, 2026

The TSX holds steady near 36,000

Canada’s main stock index closed up 0.3% at 36,265 after the rate decision, with banks gaining and several gold miners reporting earnings.

The S&P/TSX Composite is like a scoreboard for Canada’s biggest listed companies, such as banks, miners and energy firms. When it rises, those companies are worth more overall.
Source: Trading Economics ↗
Economy · USOct 28, 2026

US Federal Reserve holds rates, hints at nothing

On the same day, the Fed kept rates unchanged but gave no clear signal on whether it might raise them in December.

The Fed is the US version of the Bank of Canada. Because the two economies are closely linked, a US decision can move Canadian markets and the Canadian dollar too.
Source: Trading Economics ↗
BusinessOct 27, 2026

Celestica jumps nearly 12% on strong results

The Toronto tech manufacturer beat third-quarter expectations and gave an upbeat revenue outlook for 2027, bouncing back from losses the week before.

Companies report results every three months. When results and forecasts beat what investors expected, the share price often jumps. When they miss, it can fall just as fast.
Source: Trading Economics ↗
Markets · USNov 18, 2026

S&P 500 slips to its lowest close since July

US stocks had four losing days in a row, though tech futures recovered the next morning as worries about AI spending eased.

When US stocks fall, Canadian stocks often follow, since many Canadian firms sell to or invest in the US. A losing streak shows investors are nervous, not that anything is broken.
Source: StockAnalysis ↗
MarketsNov 4, 2026

Stocks rally as oil falls more than 8%

Oil prices dropped sharply in one day and the Dow jumped, according to CNBC’s reporting.

Canada is a big oil producer, so oil prices matter here. Falling oil can ease gas prices for drivers, but it can lower the profits of energy companies on the TSX.
